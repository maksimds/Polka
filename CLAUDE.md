# Polka

Personal macOS menu bar app (Swift/SwiftUI + AppKit) that turns the MacBook notch into a hover-to-open shelf for files and links. Hover the notch to see stored items, drag files or links onto it to store them, drag items out into any app. Personal use, private repo.

`PolkaPRD.md` is the source of truth for goals, scope, requirements (FR-1 to FR-17), the data model, risks, and milestones. Read it before planning feature work.

## Settled decisions

- Dev machine: macOS 15.7.4, Xcode 16.3. Code is edited in VS Code; Xcode is for project settings and debugging.
- App deployment target: macOS 14.0. Swift 5 language mode. Bundle ID `com.maksimds.Polka`, personal team signing.
- Unsandboxed regular app with a Dock icon (it was a menu-bar-only agent, `LSUIElement = YES`, until 2026-11-12) and a `MenuBarExtra` Quit item. It's in the Dock and Cmd-Tab at all times. A click on the Dock icon only makes Polka the active app, which shows its menu bar (Polka, Edit, View, Window, Help); it opens no window. The Polka menu has Settings… (Cmd-comma), added with `CommandGroup(replacing: .appSettings)` since Settings isn't a SwiftUI `Settings` scene. Dropping files on the Dock icon isn't supported yet.
- Launch at login is an "Open at Login" checkmark in the menu bar menu and a switch in Settings, both backed by one `LoginItem` using `SMAppService.mainApp`. It's off until turned on. macOS keeps the setting (it's Polka's row in System Settings > Login Items), so Polka reads it again whenever a menu opens or Polka becomes the active app.
- Settings is an AppKit window (`SettingsWindowController`) holding a SwiftUI grouped form, opened from the menu bar menu ("Settings…", Cmd-comma) or the gear at the expanded panel's top right, which collapses the panel first. It is not a SwiftUI `Settings` scene, so Polka controls how it comes forward and goes away. It opens on the pointer's screen, and closing it hands focus back to the app you were in. Polka sizes it (`fit`): as tall as the whole form, measured once on a copy that doesn't scroll, or as tall as the screen's visible area allows less 8 pt at each end, with the form scrolling (bounce only when it overflows). It opens a little above the middle but never past the menu bar or the screen's bottom. The color picker made the form 871 pt tall, more than a 13-inch MacBook Air at its larger-text setting shows (845 pt).
- The panel follows the pointer. There is one panel, and it moves to whatever screen the pointer is on while it's collapsed or open as a drop zone, but never while expanded or during a drag out. A screen without a notch gets the top-center pill (FR-14).
- The panel is drawn with `NotchShape`: rounded bottom corners, and top corners that curve outward into the screen edge (14 pt open). On a notch screen the closed shape has no outward curves and hides inside the notch. On a screen without one it's a 160 pt pill as tall as the menu bar, with a round bottom and 6 pt outward curves.
- Glass, after iOS 27's Liquid Glass (since 2026-11-12), drawn by hand in `UI/Glass.swift` because `glassEffect` needs macOS 26: a dark see-through body, a little lighter at the top, and a sharp 0.75 pt rim of light, brightest at the top, faint down the sides, with a reflection along the bottom. These pieces don't blur, since the panel is a transparent window and a blur there would show the desktop, not the panel; the panel's own background can blur instead (next bullet). No moving highlights. With Reduce Transparency on, glass turns solid grey and glass letters solid white. Glass is on the header's symbols and title, the tiles' remove badges and pins, the message's ×, the link field's Add button, link icons and the drop zone's tray; file thumbnails, the eyes, the dashed box, the menu bar icon and the app icon are left as they are.
- Transparency (FR-22, since 2026-11-13): Settings has an "Appearance" section with a "Transparency" slider, 0 to 100% in 5% steps (`panelTransparencyPercent`, 0 by default, shown as "Solid"), at the user's request a slider rather than a switch. Above 0 the open panel (shelf, drop zone, link field) is glass all the way up (`PanelBackground`): AppKit's material view (`NSVisualEffectView`, `.sidebar` as in Finder, blending `.behindWindow`) blurs what's behind the window, and black lies over it at 1 minus the setting, so 100% is the blurred material alone. The hardware notch then shows as a black block at the top middle, and the menu bar shows through, blurred, beside it; the user chose that over black fading into glass at the top. The material is always dark (`darkAqua`, since everything on the shelf is white) and always `.active` (otherwise it turns flat grey while Polka isn't the active app, which is nearly always). A rim of light runs down the sides and along the bottom, none along the top, which is the screen's edge, as strong as the setting. The closed notch stays solid black, and the black thins and thickens with the shape's spring. The shadow is drawn from an opaque copy of the shape with the shape cut out (`destinationOut`), so it falls only outside and doesn't show through the glass. Reduce Transparency keeps the panel solid, greys out the slider and says why in the footnote. At 0 there's no material view at all. The Settings window stays a standard window.
- Panel color (FR-24, since 2026-11-13): Appearance has a "Color" picker like a web color picker (the swatches it started with were replaced the same day, at the user's request, after a screenshot of Google's): a square of saturation (across) and brightness (down) for the hue picked on a rainbow bar under it, each with a round thumb ringed in white, and on the square's left a preview block (92 pt) showing the shelf as it will look, the deepened gradient with the sheen and "Polka" in glass letters, not the raw color. The very bottom of the square is black, the panel as it always was. The picker keeps its own hue, saturation and brightness (`PanelColor(hue:saturation:brightness:)`, `hsb`), so they survive black and greys, which have none, and 8-bit rounding; it stores the code on every drag and ignores its own change coming back. Under it is a "Hex" field, at the user's request with no system color picker. The field takes "#2881DB", "2881db" or "#28D", applies on Return or a click anywhere else in the window, turns red and changes nothing for a code it can't read, and goes back to the current code when emptied. Stored as the code under `panelColor` (empty, unreadable or #000000 means Black). The color is never drawn as it is: `PanelColor.gradient` turns it 14° around the color wheel each way and deepens both ends, to at most relative luminance 0.06 and to at least 55% of its brightness (so white names keep 9.5:1 or better), the end a further 36%, and the panel draws it from the top left to the bottom right (style C of three the user compared), with a soft sheen across the top and the rim of light. It takes the black's place over the blur: at Solid it's opaque, and Transparency thins it, down to 30% at 100% (`PanelBackground.leastTint`), so the color still shows. The closed notch stays black. Reduce Transparency keeps the gradient, opaque.
- The open panel's strip beside the notch shows "Polka" at the top left in glass letters (`GlassTitle`, 15 pt bold, half gloss: `GlassGlyph`'s `gloss` 0.5) and the Settings gear at the top right, both 2 pt below the middle of the notch's height (`NotchView.headerDrop`, shared with the link field's header). The strip is laid out on the tiles' grid (`ShelfGridView.columns`, the same 16 pt side inset, each end held to the 52 pt picture square `ShelfItemTile.imageSide`), so "Polka" starts where the first column's pictures start and the gear ends where the last column's pictures end. Left of the gear are the sort button (`arrow.up.arrow.down`) and then the + button (`plus`). Each of the three is a glass symbol itself, with no circle behind it (`GlassHeaderSymbol`, 14 pt semibold, gloss 0.85), 16 pt apart. The three end at that picture square's right edge and run a little into the fourth column, still clear of the notch. While any sort is on, the sort symbol is blue glass, #2881DB (`SortMenuButton.activeColor`, a fixed blue, not the system accent). Each frame is just the symbol, with an 8 pt larger click area, so the areas meet without overlapping.
- The shelf sorts like a Finder window's "Sort By". The sort button opens a menu headed "Sort By" with None, Name, Kind, File Type and Date Added, a checkmark on the current one, stored under `shelfSort` (None by default). Name is A to Z by the tile's name (`displayName`, so a name given with Rename counts), Finder style (case doesn't count, "file2" before "file10"); Kind is folders, then files, then links, then text, A to Z within each; File Type is folders, then one group per file extension from A to Z ("pdf" and "PDF" together, titled "PDF"), then files with no extension ("Other"), then links, then text, A to Z within each; Date Added is newest first. Ties keep their order. Kind and File Type show their groups: each starts a new row under a title row (20 pt: the name in 11 pt semibold dim white, then a hairline to the end of the row), the title's ends lined up with the first and last columns' pictures like the header strip. 2 pt from a title to its tiles, 10 pt from a group's last row to the next title. Titles take no presses, so clicks and selection rectangles work there as on free space. Any choice but None keeps the shelf sorted through every change (new items land in their sorted spot, a file renamed on disk, a tile renamed with Rename, or a link's arriving site name moves it), and tiles can't be moved by hand: the others don't make room, and a drop on the shelf glides the picture back to its own spot. Dragging out works as usual. Back on None, the tiles stay where the last sort put them, as in Finder, and new items arrive at the top left again, after any pinned tiles. After a drop, the panel opens scrolled to show the new item if it sorted out of view.
- The + button opens a menu headed "Add New" with Link and File, then a divider and Paste (next bullet), placed like Sort By. Link turns the shelf into a link field (`NotchState.addingLink`): the shape shrinks with the opening spring to the shelf's width and just tall enough for the field (`linkEntrySize`), with "Add Link" where "Polka" was (glass letters too) and a glass × symbol where the gear was. The field (placeholder `https://example.com`) has its ends lined up with the first and last columns' pictures, and its Add button is clear glass with a dim label, turning #2881DB glass once the text is a web address. The menu items have no "…", at the user's request. `LinkInput` reads the text: no scheme means https (so "example.com" works, but then the host needs a dot or must be "localhost"), only http and https are accepted, and the scheme and host are lowercased and an empty path made "/", as browsers write them, so it matches the same page dragged in. Return adds it like a dropped link and grows the field back into the shelf, scrolled to the new tile. Text that isn't an address, or a link already on the shelf, gets a red line under the field, and the field stays. Esc, the ×, or a click in another app goes back without adding anything; moving the pointer away doesn't close it. File collapses the panel and opens the standard Open dialog (`NSOpenPanel`: files and folders, several at once, button "Add"). What's chosen is stored like dropped files, duplicates get the usual message, and the next hover opens scrolled to them.
- Paste (FR-19, since 2026-11-13) is the + menu's last item, named after what's on the clipboard as the menu opens (`PasteReader.contents`): "Paste File", "Paste 3 Files", "Paste Folder", "Paste 2 Items", "Paste Image", "Paste Link", "Paste Text", or a greyed-out "Paste" when there's nothing Polka can keep. It shows no ⌘V and isn't one: the panel never takes the keys, so the shortcut couldn't reach it. Copied files come first and are stored like dropped ones. Then a picture, but only when the text that comes with it is nothing or just an address (a browser's "Copy Image"); text with a picture of itself (Word, Numbers) is text. Then links: the clipboard's `.URL`, or text that's nothing but an http or https address. Then text, without blank lines above it or space after it but with its first line's indent. The shelf stays open and scrolls to what was added (`ShelfGridView` follows `revealItemID` while open). A picture's bytes are read only once it's chosen, then saved in the background into a copy folder as "Pasted Image 2026-11-13 at 10.42.15.png" (PNG, JPEG, GIF and HEIC as they came, anything else, TIFF or PDF included, made into a PNG at its own dpi), a copy with no original like a promised file, deleted when removed, never checked for duplicates.
- Text is an item kind of its own (`.text`, its text in shelf.json as `Source.text`, at most 200,000 UTF-16 units, `ShelfItem.maxTextLength`; past that the panel says "That text is too long to keep on the shelf"). It comes from Paste or a drop. It's named after its first line with something on it, spaces squeezed, cut at 60 characters with "…", or "Text"; its type line says "Text"; its name is cut off at the end like a link's. Its picture is a small white letter-shaped page (40 × 52 pt, `ShelfItemImages.textPage`) with the start of the text in 5.5 pt grey, drawn on demand, so its remove badge sits on the page's corner like a document's. The same text twice is a duplicate. Dragged out (or copied with Copy) it's plain text; Finder makes a text clipping of it. Quick Look and double-click write it to a temporary file named after the tile (`TextPreviewFile`, in macOS's temporary folder, marked UTF-8 with the `com.apple.TextEncoding` attribute so Cyrillic shows right), since Quick Look only shows files; Get Info leaves it out.
- Screenshots (FR-20, since 2026-11-13): Settings has a "Screenshots" section with "Save Screenshots to Polka", off by default. On, it sets macOS's own screenshot save location (`location` in `com.apple.screencapture`, what the Screenshot toolbar's Options sets) to "Polka Screenshots" in Polka's folder, and remembers the old one under `screenshotsPreviousLocation` (empty for macOS's default, the Desktop); off puts that back. Whether it's on is read from macOS's setting, again whenever Settings opens, so picking another location in the toolbar turns it off. Polka never watches the Desktop, so macOS asks for no permission. A dispatch source watches the folder; a file that has sat unchanged for 0.5 s (hidden files skipped) is moved into its own copy folder and added newest first as a temporary copy with no original (`origin: screenshot`), lasting the current temporary lifetime. Screen recordings come the same way. Files that arrived while Polka wasn't running are taken at launch. The next hover opens scrolled to them; the open shelf doesn't jump. When one expires or is removed, its file goes to the Trash. The footnote names where screenshots go without Polka, in one sentence for both states so the window doesn't change height. macOS saves a screenshot only once its floating thumbnail goes away, so one arrives about 5 s after it's taken.
- Motion: opening is one spring (0.42 s, bounce 0.12) shared by the shape and the shelf, which scales up from 0.92 at the top and fades in, so it grows out of the notch as one piece; closing is a quicker spring without bounce, with the shelf fading out first; the closed notch swells 14 × 4 pt in the last 100 ms of the hover delay with the same spring, so it flows into the opening (started earlier, it read as two separate steps); the open panel's shadow fades in once the opening settles; tiles grow in as the grid reflows, and a removed one turns to dust (next bullet); a moved tile's neighbors slide aside once the pointer rests on a spot, and its dropped picture glides into its new spot; the delete button and selection fade; the drop zone has a glass tray icon (26 pt) that bounces once when a drag arrives and brightens while a drag is over the panel, and its eyes blink and grow 10% while a drag is over the panel. With Reduce Motion on, all of it becomes plain fades and the notch doesn't swell; tiles making room for a moved one jump to their places, a dropped tile fades in, a removed tile fades out instead of turning to dust, and the eyes still follow the pointer but don't blink or grow.
- A tile removed from the open shelf (its ×, or Remove, one tile or a selection) turns to dust, after Telegram's delete animation (since 2026-11-12), in its classic version (2023 to Oct 2026), not the newer one with air currents and glitter. A picture of what's drawn of the tile (picture, name and type line, a selection's highlight included, the × not) breaks into grains of one point each. A front crosses it from left to right in 0.8 s; each grain it reaches bursts off in a random direction at 42 to 84 pt/s, is pulled upward like smoke and fades out, about 1.55 s in all (simulation time runs twice as fast as real time, as in Telegram). The dust is drawn with Metal (`DustEffect.metal`) over the whole panel, header included, and only inside its outline; a tile half scrolled out of sight only turns its visible part to dust. The other tiles close the gap over 0.8 s (`ShelfGridView.dustGapDuration`) instead of the usual 0.28 s, so the shelf settles as the dust rises. Closing the panel fades the dust out in 0.1 s. Without Metal, or in tests, tiles leave at once and shrink away as before. Expired items leave while the panel is closed, so they get no dust.
- The app icon is the menu bar's `tray.full` symbol (semibold) as milky white glass on a dark graphite macOS tile (since 2026-11-12; it was near-black glass on a white tile before), at half gloss (`gloss` 0.5 in the script; full gloss looked like polished chrome): bright light reflections along the top of each stroke, grey edges where the glass reflects the dark room, a darker reflection of the tile along the bottom, and a soft shadow under it. `Design/make-app-icon.swift` draws it and writes every size into `AppIcon.appiconset`, so change the script and run it again rather than editing the PNGs.
- Storage is a plain JSON file for v1, not SwiftData.
- Hybrid storage: items reference the original by default, with an optional "keep a copy" for permanent items.
- Deleting an item never deletes the original file.
- New items are permanent by default. Temporary is opt-in per item, with the "Temporary" checkmark in the tile's right-click menu (the clock button in the tile's corner was removed), and the tile's detail line shows the time left. It lasts 24 hours unless changed in Settings (1, 6 or 12 hours, 1 or 3 days, 1 week), stored under `temporaryLifetimeSeconds`. A new lifetime only applies to items made temporary afterwards. A value set by hand with `defaults write` shows up as an extra choice.
- Pin (FR-21, since 2026-11-13): every tile's right-click menu has "Pin", reading "Unpin" on a pinned tile as in Notes and Messages, first in the group with Temporary, acting on the tile alone. Pinned tiles (`pinned` in shelf.json, nil when not) have the first row to themselves in every sort, at most five (`ShelfStore.maxPinned`, one row). Pin on a sixth pins nothing and says "You can pin up to 5 items. Unpin one to pin this.", rather than a greyed-out item that doesn't say why. Within the row they follow the current sort (`ShelfSort.arranged` sorts the pinned and the rest separately); unsorted, a newly pinned tile goes to the end of the row and an unpinned one becomes the first after it, where new items arrive too. The rest start on the next row, 10 pt lower (`groupSpacing`), with no title: `ShelfSort.groups(of:)` makes the pinned tiles an untitled group (`ShelfGroup.title` nil), followed by the order's titled groups, or by the rest as one untitled group. Each pinned tile has a glass pin (`GlassPinBadge`: `pin.fill`, 11 pt semibold, upright, the header symbols' gloss, with a red tint, #FF6B5E, `GlassPinBadge.tint`, for visibility; #FFA39E read as pink) over the top-left corner of the picture you can see, its middle 2 pt in, mirroring the remove badge (lower on a folder, the same way), with a soft dark halo so it still shows over a white page. It's part of the tile's face, so it turns to dust with the tile and dims with an unavailable file. On an unsorted shelf, tiles moved by hand stay in their part: held over the other part, they wait at the nearest end of their own (`NotchViewModel.target`, from `ShelfGridLayout.spot(at:in:)`, which knows each row's group), and pinned and unpinned tiles moved together don't make room and glide back.
- The drop zone has a pair of eyes just under the notch, above the dashed box (moved down to make room): 9.9 pt below the notch, then the eyes, then 11.88 pt down to the box. They're drawn after a face on a ball: two solid white capsules (12.72 × 43.2 pt, 28 pt apart) with no pupils, so looking means the whole face turning. Toward the pointer, the pair slides up to 6 × 3 pt, the eyes come closer together, the eye on that side shrinks a little, and a diagonal look tilts the head. A pointer 150 pt away turns them halfway. They blink every 2 s, the first a second after the drop zone opens, so a short drag still sees one. They read the pointer every frame (`TimelineView`), since no mouse-moved events arrive during a drag.
- An incoming drag from another app opens the drop zone after 0.5 s, or right away when the pointer reaches the notch's hover area (the Settings hover area). A drag that ends sooner never opens it. Opening instantly on every drag was distracting.
- Items stay on the shelf after being dragged out.
- Tiles can be moved around the shelf like apps on an iPhone home screen. Dragging a tile lifts it out, leaving a gap. Once the pointer rests on a spot for 150 ms, the other tiles slide aside to open the gap there, so sweeping across the grid doesn't shuffle every tile it passes. Every spot stays on the 5-column grid. A drop on the shelf puts the tile in the spot under the pointer, even if the tiles haven't made room yet, and saves the order (`sortOrder` in shelf.json). Dragging past the panel's edge makes it a normal drag out: the panel shrinks and the shelf goes back to its old order. Esc cancels. A selection moves as a group and lands side by side in its old order. New items still arrive at the top left, after any pinned tiles; pinned tiles and the others each move only within their own part (see Pin). All of this is only while the shelf isn't sorted (Sort By None). Held within 16 pt of the grid's top or bottom edge (or past it, on the header), the grid waits 300 ms, then scrolls one row every 450 ms while there's more that way.
- The grid has 6 pt of room above its first row (`ShelfGridView.topInset`), outside the measured grid so the spot math is unchanged. A tall picture's remove badge rises above its tile, and on an unsorted shelf, with no group title above the first row, the scroll view's top edge cut it off. Two rows still fit without scrolling. Opening the panel and scrolling a row at a time go to the very top (`topID`) for the first row, rather than lining the first tile up with the edge, which would scroll the room away.
- File tiles use Quick Look's plain thumbnail. A dark drawing on a transparent background (at least 20% see-through, visible part at most 0.35 brightness, measured on a 32 × 32 copy) would vanish on the black shelf, so it's asked for again in Quick Look's icon mode, which draws it on a white page with a thin border like Finder. Icon mode isn't used for everything because it also frames photos.
- Hover is tuned in Settings: "Open after" (0 to 800 ms in 50 ms steps, 250 by default, stored as `hoverDelayMilliseconds`) and "Hover area" (Small is just the notch and is the default, Medium adds 12 pt at the sides and 4 below, Large adds 32 and 12; stored as `hoverArea`). Every area keeps 4 pt above the notch. Changes apply on the next hover.
- Multi-select works like Finder's icon view (FR-10): click selects one tile, Command-click adds or removes, Shift-click selects the range from the last plain click, a click on empty space clears, and a drag on empty space draws a selection rectangle. The rectangle selects every tile whose picture or name it touches, live; on its own it replaces the selection, with Shift it adds, with Command it flips what it covers. It stays inside the grid's visible area and doesn't scroll the grid. Dragging a selected tile carries the whole selection (missing files stay behind); dragging an unselected tile selects it alone. Selected tiles get a grey square behind the picture and an accent capsule around the name. Collapsing the panel clears the selection.
- A tile is pressed and hovered like a desktop icon: only what's drawn of it counts (`tileShape`), exactly, with no slack: the visible part of its picture, its name and its type line, each as wide as it's drawn, with the small gaps between them bridged so moving down the tile doesn't leave it. A link's icon or card counts whole; a file's thumbnail or icon counts without the see-through room a folder or document icon leaves around its drawing (`ShelfItemImages.visibleBounds`, measured once per image on a 64 × 64 copy). The rest of its column is free space, where a click clears the selection, a drag starts a selection rectangle, and the pointer doesn't hover the tile. Hover shows the remove badge, and while it shows, the badge's own area (26 pt with its margin) keeps the tile hovered, so the pointer can reach it where it hangs past the picture. The badge is the one iPhone puts on an app being deleted, with a × instead of a minus (`GlassRemoveBadge`: a 20 pt light grey glass circle with a dark ×, and a soft shadow), hanging over the top-right corner of the picture you can see, its middle 4 pt in from that corner; a tall page gets it on the page's corner, not the empty side of the picture square, and a folder gets it 15% of the picture's height lower (`folderBadgeDrop`), since the folder icon leaves the top of its square empty above the tab. During a trackpad scroll, tiles sliding under the resting pointer don't count as hovered, as in Finder; the tile under the pointer shows its × once the scroll comes to rest.
- Double-click opens an item (FR-9): a file in its default app, a folder in Finder, a link in the default browser. The panel collapses first. A file that can't be found does nothing. Text has nothing to open, so it shows in Quick Look instead.
- Opening, Quick Look, Get Info and Share use a kept copy's original while it can still be found, since that's the real file, and Polka's copy otherwise.
- The right-click menu for files and folders is Quick Look, Get Info, Rename, Share, a divider, Pin, Temporary and Keep a Copy (FR-16), then, for a document, a divider and Convert to PDF (FR-18), then a divider and Remove. Links get Rename, Share, a divider, Pin, Temporary, a divider and Remove. Text gets Quick Look, Copy, Rename, Share, a divider, Pin, Temporary, a divider and Remove; Copy puts its text on the clipboard and acts on the tile alone. Copy and Share are the two choices that don't collapse the panel. Rename sits right after Get Info, as in Finder, and acts on the tile alone. It's "Remove", not "Delete", because it only takes items off the shelf and never sends a file to the Trash, as with "Remove from Dock". Quick Look, Get Info, Share and Remove act on the tile, or on the whole selection if the tile is in it; Remove then says how many ("Remove 3 Items"), since it can't be undone. Quick Look, Get Info and Share are greyed out for an unavailable file. Temporary and Keep a Copy are checkmarks that act on the tile alone. The × button on a tile also removes just that tile. Quick Look and Get Info collapse the panel first. Quick Look is the system `QLPreviewPanel` (Space or Esc closes it, arrows move through several files). Get Info is Finder's own "Show Info" service, which needs no permission, unlike asking Finder through AppleScript.
- Share (FR-23, since 2026-11-13) is macOS's own share popover (`NSSharingServicePicker`, the one Finder's Share… shows: AirDrop, Mail, Messages, Notes, people you share with often, Edit Extensions), from `ShareController`. The menu item reads "Share", without Finder's "…", like Polka's other menu items. It sends the tile, or the whole selection if the tile is in it, the tile first, with missing files left behind (`NotchViewModel.itemsToShare`): a file as the file Polka opens, a link as its web address (web addresses only, as for opening), text as plain text (`ShareController.objects(for:)`). The popover points at the tile as drawn (picture, name and type line, `TileDragView.drawnArea`), from below where there's room. Polka activates for it, as for Quick Look, since Messages', Notes' and Reminders' share windows are drawn in Polka's own process. The shelf stays open, as under a menu, while the popover and then the service picked from it are up (`ShareController.isSharing`). That ends when the popover is dismissed, the service says it shared or failed (cancelling counts as failing), or Polka stops being the active app (a fallback for a service that never says, or Mail taking over); then focus goes back to the app you were in and the shelf collapses if the pointer is away.
- Rename (FR-17, added 2026-11-12) works like renaming an icon in Finder, but only changes the tile's label: the file keeps its name and path, a drag out drops the real file with its real name, and a link keeps its address. The name turns into a field in place (`TileNameEditor`, over the name, which is hidden meanwhile): a dark box with the system focus ring, as wide as what's typed, up to the tile's width less 8 pt so the ring isn't clipped at the grid's edge, then scrolling. It starts with the name selected, a file's without its extension ("report" of "report.pdf"; a given name only loses an extension it really ends with). The tile becomes the only one selected and stays selected after. Return or Tab keeps what's typed, Esc keeps the old name, and a click anywhere else keeps what's typed: elsewhere in the panel (another tile, free space, the header) or in another app, which also collapses the panel. The pointer leaving doesn't close the panel while renaming. The name is stored as `customName` in shelf.json (nil means the automatic name), and the tile, Sort By Name and messages use `displayName` (`customName ?? title`). `title` keeps following the file and the site underneath, so the given name survives the file being renamed or moved on disk, a link's site name arriving, and Keep a Copy either way. An empty name, or the automatic one, clears `customName` and brings the automatic name back; spaces around are trimmed and line breaks become spaces. Two tiles can share a name. A missing file can be renamed too.
- Convert to PDF (FR-18, added 2026-11-12) is in the right-click menu of a document tile (`PDFConversion`: docx, doc, rtf, rtfd, odt, and .txt itself, not code or Markdown), in its own group above Remove. It acts on the whole selection if the tile is in it ("Convert 3 Items to PDF", counting only documents that can be converted now), and is greyed out while the file is unavailable or already converting, when the tile's detail line says "Converting…". The PDF is named after the file, not the tile's given name ("report.pdf", then "report 2.pdf" as in Finder, never overwriting anything), saved next to the file the tile opens (a kept copy's original while it can be found), and added as a new tile right after its document (its sorted spot on a sorted shelf), permanent like any new item. The open shelf scrolls only as far as it takes to show it (`itemToShow`); a closed panel opens scrolled to it. A document that only lives in Polka's folder (a promised file, or a kept copy whose original is gone) gets its PDF kept there as a copy, and so does one whose folder can't be written to, with a message, since removing that tile deletes the PDF. Each PDF is drawn in a copy folder first and only then moved out, so a half-written one never shows up in your folders. Word files go through Quick Look's converter (`qlmanage -p -o`, Apple's tool for testing Quick Look, which writes the preview as a web page), which keeps pictures, a header (once, at the top, not on every page) and links. Everything else, and a Word file Quick Look gives nothing back for, goes through Apple's own reader (TextEdit's engine), which drops a Word file's pictures and headers. Pages take the document's own size and margins; plain text gets the Mac's paper size, inch margins and TextEdit's plain-text font (Menlo 11). TextKit cuts the text into pages, one text container per page, and draws them into a PDF with web and mail links clickable. The document itself is never changed. Saving next to a document in Desktop, Documents or Downloads may make macOS ask for access once; the reason it shows is the `INFOPLIST_KEY_NS…FolderUsageDescription` build settings.
- Drag out offers copy only, so a drop into Finder can never move the original. Over Polka's own panel the drag offers move (plus copy and generic, so holding Option or Command still moves the tile), so the pointer shows no green plus while tiles change places.
- Dropping something already on the shelf adds nothing and shows a message inside the panel. The message only closes with its × button. While it is up, the panel collapses after the pointer has been away for 3 seconds, and the message is still there on the next hover.
- Link tiles show the site's icon (from LinkPresentation, else the icons the page's head lists, Apple touch icons first, then the site's `/favicon.ico`; LinkPresentation can't load Notion's pages, and Notion has no `/favicon.ico`) with glass built in like an iOS 27 app icon (`GlassPictureFinish`: a sheen across the top and a rim of light). How it sits in its 12 pt rounded square depends on the icon (`SiteIconStyle`, worked out once per icon file by `ShelfItemImages.siteIconStyle`). A large icon (96 pt wide or more) is a tile or a bare logo, told apart on a 32 × 32 copy: if more than half of its outer ring of pixels is see-through, it's a logo. A tile (GitHub's, Яндекс's: solid to the edges) fills the square. A bare logo (Shopify's bag) sits at 34 pt on dark glass (`GlassSurface` plus the finish, quieter than the panel's other glass since 2026-11-13: the body's gradient at 0.4, the sheen at 0.5 and the rim light at 0.7, `ShelfItemTile.logoGlassGradient`, `logoGlassSheen`, `logoGlassRim`); filling the square stretched it to the edges and cut it off. A favicon (under 96 pt, like LinkedIn's) is treated as a logo, at 28 pt, and never fills the square, even a solid one, since it would blur. A logo or favicon that's dark (`needsLightBackground`) goes on the light glass card (`LightGlassCard`) instead, since dark glass would hide it. Link tiles are titled "site/address path", like `GitHub/apple/swift`. The site name is the one the page declares (`og:site_name`), otherwise the capitalized domain (`Linkedin`). Requests are never disguised as a browser: a site that refuses Polka (LinkedIn) just gets the fallbacks.
- A file whose original can't be found stays on the shelf as "Unavailable" and can't be dragged out, but it can still be moved around the shelf. Its drag carries only a private pasteboard type (`ShelfDragSource.shelfOnlyType`) that no other app reads, and the panel doesn't shrink when it leaves, so it can come back. Anything Polka deletes from disk must be inside its own Application Support folder.
- "Keep a copy" lives in the tile's right-click menu, as a checkmark item for files and folders. Checking it copies the original in the background and points the item at the copy. Unchecking it goes back to the original and deletes the copy, but only if the original can still be found; otherwise the copy stays and a message says so.
- Copies live in `Copies/<UUID>/<file name>` inside Polka's folder, one folder per copy, so a copy keeps its real name when dragged out. A copy is deleted once no item uses it (removed, expired, or back on its original), except a screenshot's, which goes to the Trash (`ShelfCopies.trashFolder`; tests swap in their own `moveToTrash`). Leftover folders are cleared at launch, except when shelf.json couldn't be read.
- Files that apps hand over as a promise (Photos, Mail, browser images) are written into a new copy folder and stored as copies with no original. They aren't checked for duplicates.
- A drop is read as real files first, then file promises, then links, then text (`.string`, since 2026-11-13). A browser image offers both its address and a promise, and the image wins. A promise of a .webloc or .url file means the link, and so does text that's nothing but an http or https address written out in full (`LinkInput.link(inText:)`); "example.com" or "notes.txt" in a document stays text.
- Links are web addresses only (since 2026-11-12, from the security scan): http or https with a host (`LinkInput.isWebAddress`), typed or dropped. A web page decides what its drags carry, and opening any other scheme (`smb:`, an app's own) would hand it to another app without the browser's "Allow this page to open…?" question. A dropped one comes back from `DropReceiver` as `refusedLink`, isn't stored, and the panel says "That link isn't a web address, so it wasn't added". `ItemOpener.open` and the preview fetcher also skip anything that isn't a file or a web address, for links saved before the rule.
- Link previews treat what a site sends as untrusted, since Polka isn't sandboxed (`LinkPreviewFetcher.Limits`): their own ephemeral `URLSession` with 15 s idle and 20 s total limits, the page read in bytes up to `</head>` or 600 KB, an icon file at most 1 MB (dropped as soon as it goes past), and at most 3 icon files tried per link when LinkPresentation finds none (`iconCandidates`: web addresses only, no `data:` or `mask-icon`). Every icon arrives as bytes and goes through `decodeIcon`: only ICO, PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP and WebP by what the bytes are, no side over 2048 px (read from the header before decoding), the largest allowed size of an .ico. SVG icons aren't decoded (ImageIO doesn't read them, and AppKit's reader is a whole XML parser), so such a site keeps the generic icon.
- Previews only contact the public internet (`LinkPreviewFetcher.mayLookUp`, `LocalNetwork`), at the user's choice, `localhost` included: no link, page-listed icon or redirect (`PublicRedirectsOnly`, the session's delegate) to this Mac or the local network is fetched. Local means private, loopback, link-local, carrier-grade NAT (Tailscale), multicast and reserved IPv4 ranges in any written form (`2130706433` is 127.0.0.1), the matching IPv6 ones, names without a dot, and names ending in `localhost`, `local`, `localdomain`, `lan`, `home`, `home.arpa` or `internal`. App Transport Security lets plain http through to exactly these, so a dropped link could otherwise reach a router the moment it lands. Such a link keeps the name from its address and the generic icon, and opens as usual.
- Logs keep what's yours private (`.private`, shown as `<private>`): item and file names, paths, web addresses and site names, other apps' names, and error messages, which macOS words with file names. Public are only Polka's workings: counts, states, pasteboard types, link schemes, and each error's domain and code (`Error.logCode`). Any admin can read public log entries, and they go into diagnostic reports.
- The Xcode project uses synchronized folders: new files under `Polka/Polka/` join the target automatically. Don't hand-edit `project.pbxproj` unless necessary.
- `buildServer.json` and `.vscode/settings.json` are local-only and git-ignored.
- The daily copy is `/Applications/Polka.app`, a Release build. The Desktop alias and Open at Login both launch it. Builds from Xcode or `xcodebuild` go to DerivedData and never update it, so `install.sh` at the repo root rebuilds it (into the git-ignored `Polka/build/`), replaces it, re-registers it with Launch Services (`lsregister -f`) so Finder and the Dock show its current icon instead of a saved one, and relaunches it. Open at Login points at whichever copy turned it on, so turn it on from the installed copy, not a Debug build.

## AppKit lessons (don't undo these)

- The panel can't become key (`canBecomeKey` returns `NotchPanel.acceptsKeyboard`, normally false). That is how it never steals focus. There are two exceptions, both at the user's request: the link field (`.addingLink`) and a tile being renamed (`renamingItemID` set). The controller watches both and turns it on and makes the panel key for either. A `.nonactivatingPanel` can be the key window without activating Polka, so the other app keeps its menu bar. While it's key, the panel handles Cmd-X, C, V, A, Z and Shift-Z itself and swallows every other Command shortcut, since Polka's menus aren't on screen (Cmd-Q would quit a Polka you can't see). Leaving the field hands the keys back by ordering the panel out and straight back in (`giveKeyboardBack`); losing them to a click elsewhere collapses the panel. When a click elsewhere in the panel ended a rename, the hand-back waits until no mouse button is held, no tile drag is running and no menu is open (the run loop isn't in `.eventTracking`), since taking the panel off screen in the middle of that press would cut it short. Don't make the panel key for anything else.
- While a tile is being renamed, `NotchPanel.sendEvent` ends the edit (`makeFirstResponder(nil)`, which keeps what's typed) on any press outside the field, before the press does anything else. Clicking a view that can't take typing, like a tile, wouldn't end it on its own. The field being edited is found as the delegate of the field editor, the text view AppKit lends a field while it's edited. The rename field is AppKit (`NSTextField`), not a SwiftUI `TextField`, because picking what's selected when editing starts needs macOS 15 in SwiftUI. It sits in an overlay above the tile's drag layer, so presses in it place the caret instead of pressing the tile.
- SwiftUI `.onDrop` does not work here because it tries to make the panel key. Drops go through the AppKit `DropTargetView`.
- Never show an `NSAlert` or other system dialog from the panel, because it would activate Polka and take focus. Messages go inside the panel (`NoticeBanner`).
- Settings, Quick Look, Share and the Open dialog (`FileChooser`) are the only times Polka activates itself (`NSApp.activate()`), because a window from an inactive app opens behind the app you're in and can't take keystrokes. All are windows you asked for, and the panel collapses before Quick Look or the Open dialog opens. Share's popover points at a tile, so the panel stays open under it instead. Never activate Polka for the panel itself, the link field and renaming included. Opening an item or Get Info activates the other app (or Finder), never Polka.
- Quick Look finds the object that supplies its files by asking down the responder chain, starting at the key window. Polka never has a key window there, so the question reaches the app delegate. With SwiftUI that's SwiftUI's own delegate, which passes it on to `AppDelegate` (checked by `OpenAndInspectTests.theAppDelegateAnswersQuickLook`), which passes it to `QuickLookController`.
- AppKit puts the caret in a window's first text field when it opens, which in Settings is the hex code, so `SettingsWindowController.show` clears the first responder on opening, at once and again a run-loop pass later for SwiftUI (checked with a harness around the real `SettingsView`). A click anywhere but the field ends the typing (`SettingsWindow.sendEvent`, like the panel's rename), since AppKit keeps a field going when you click a control that takes no typing, such as the color picker. A grouped form draws text fields without a border, so the hex field is `.roundedBorder`, or it reads as a label.
- The Settings window handles Cmd-W itself, since Polka's menu bar has no File menu and so no Close item.
- When Settings, Quick Look, Share or the Open dialog closes, `FocusReturner` hands focus back with `NSRunningApplication.activate(from: .current)` to the last other app that was active. Otherwise Polka stays active with no window to type into. Quick Look, Share and the Open dialog skip this while Settings is open (Share also while Quick Look is).
- Any `NSWindow` that Swift keeps a reference to needs `isReleasedWhenClosed = false`, or closing it frees it twice and crashes.
- SwiftUI `Button` and tap gestures do work in the never-key panel. Only dragging needs AppKit.
- The panel is a fixed-size transparent window, and SwiftUI animates the shape inside it. `ignoresMouseEvents` is true only while the shape is notch-sized (`.collapsed` or `.draggingOut`), so clicks pass through. It also removes the window as a drop target, which is what lets a drag reach whatever is behind the panel.
- Inside drag callbacks, read the pasteboard right away but defer state changes by one run-loop tick. Changing state during the callback produces the system log "Reentrant message: kDragIPCCompleted ...".
- A file promise is the one exception: `receivePromisedFiles` is called inside `performDragOperation`, while the drag is still going. It only starts receiving; items are added when the files arrive.
- The tile's right-click menu comes from `TileDragView.menu(for:)`, because that AppKit layer covers the tile's picture and text and is what receives the click there. A right-click on the blank rest of the tile shows no menu, as on the desktop.
- The "Sort By" and "Add New" menus are `NSMenu`s opened by an AppKit layer over the header symbol (`HeaderMenuView`, in `HeaderMenuButton`), on mouse-down, not a SwiftUI `Menu`, so Polka places them: right under the symbol with the menu's right edge lined up with the symbol's. Their items run closures through `NSMenuItem(title:action:)`, which keeps its target alive in `representedObject`, since an item holds its target weakly. Opened to the right, it would hang past the panel's edge, and the panel collapses once the pointer has been out there. Menus don't need a key window, so they work in the never-key panel. The header sits in the menu bar's strip, so "right under the symbol" is still inside the menu bar: opened by a click, AppKit slides such a menu down below it, but opened by a held press (as on mouse-down) it can trim the menu's window instead, cutting off the last item behind a scroll arrow. So `HeaderMenuView` asks for no higher than just under the menu bar (`visibleFrame.maxY` less the 5 pt a menu's window starts above the point it's opened at), which looks the same.
- While any menu is open (the header's or a tile's right-click menu), the shelf doesn't collapse, wherever the pointer goes: the controller follows `NSMenu.didBeginTrackingNotification` and `didEndTrackingNotification` (delivered as posted, `queue: nil`). Mouse moves still reach the controller during menu tracking, so without this the shelf collapsed under an open menu and left it floating. Once the menu closes, the next move away collapses the shelf as usual.
- A tile click is a press that ends without moving 4 pt, handled in `TileDragView.mouseUp` with the modifier keys read at mouse-down. A double-click is the second one, by `event.clickCount`, so it uses the double-click speed from System Settings.
- The tile's AppKit layer only takes presses on the tile's shape: its `hitTest` returns nil elsewhere, and AppKit then passes the press to whatever is behind, the grid's SwiftUI tap and drag gestures. Hover doesn't go through `hitTest`, and a tracking area is a rectangle, so it covers the whole column with `.mouseMoved` as well, and each move is checked against the shape; SwiftUI is told only when the answer changes. The panel's `acceptsMouseMovedEvents` is what lets those moves arrive while Polka isn't active. The tile measures its picture, name and type line with `onGeometryChange`, and passes its hover state back down (`isHoverShown`), since SwiftUI clears it on its own when the panel closes.
- Tiles report where their picture and text are into a plain class (`PressAreas`), not SwiftUI state. Those frames change on every scroll and nothing is drawn from them, so state would redraw the grid for nothing.
- Drags out start in the AppKit `TileDragView`. `ShelfDragSource` belongs to the controller rather than a tile, so AppKit always has a live source to report to.
- Polka must not treat its own drags as incoming: `GlobalDragMonitor` ignores them (`beginOwnDrag`, `endOwnDrag`). `DropTargetView` never stores a drag with a `draggingSource`, which AppKit only sets for drags from the same app; a drag from `ShelfDragSource` is a tile being moved instead.
- Moving tiles is one AppKit drag that does both jobs, not a custom drag inside the panel handed off to AppKit at its edge. Drag out starts exactly as before, and a drop back on the panel means "move here", as in Finder.
- The spot under the pointer comes from the grid's fixed measurements (`ShelfGridLayout`), not from where tiles are drawn. Tiles slide during the preview, and measuring targets that move makes them jump back and forth.
- The grid is drawn by a custom SwiftUI `Layout` (`ShelfGridArrangement`) from those same numbers, not by `LazyVGrid`, so what's drawn always matches the math, with or without group titles. It's one layout for every order (titles and tiles are siblings, told apart by `ShelfGridEntryKey`), so a tile stays the same view when the sort changes and slides to its new spot; `LazyVGrid` sections would rebuild every tile that changes group. Its cache remembers each view's last spot, and `updateCache` is deliberately empty, so a removed tile or title fades out where it was. Unlike `LazyVGrid`, it makes every tile (and starts its thumbnail) up front; fine for a personal shelf, which is built once at launch. The grid reports where it is (`ShelfGridGeometry`, a plain class like `PressAreas`) in SwiftUI's `.global` space, which in the panel is the hosting view, measured from the top left; `DropTargetView` converts AppKit's bottom-left points to it and back.
- `draggingUpdated` keeps arriving while the pointer holds still, which is what lets the preview settle and edge scrolling carry on without mouse movement.
- A dropped picture glides into place with `animatesToDestination`, set in `prepareForDragOperation`, then each dragging item's `draggingFrame` set in `performDragOperation` (enumerated as `NSPasteboardItem`, which visits every item, in the order the drag started with). Those frames are worked out during the drop without changing any state; the tiles move a tick later and stay hidden for 220 ms while the pictures land.
- The shelf's content is built once and stays mounted at full size in every state: hidden (opacity 0, no hit testing) and clipped to the notch unless `.expanded`. Opening only reveals it, so the first frames never wait for views, icons or thumbnails to be made, and the tile a drag out started from still exists when the drag ends. Because tiles outlive a closed panel, each tile clears its hover state when the panel closes, and the grid scrolls back to its first items on opening.
- Do no disk work as the panel opens. `prepareToOpen()` (remove expired items, refresh file references) runs when the hover delay starts and when an incoming drag starts, not when the drop zone finally opens.
- No mouse-moved events arrive during a drag, so check where the pointer is when one ends.
- Tracking areas need `.activeAlways`, since Polka is rarely the active app.
- Tracking areas report enter and exit when content scrolls under a pointer that isn't moving. `TileDragView` ignores entering between `NSScrollView`'s `willStartLiveScroll` and `didEndLiveScroll` notifications (a trackpad scroll, momentum included), then checks the pointer once. A mouse wheel posts neither, so hover still changes under one.
- Inside SwiftUI's scroll view, a tile's AppKit `visibleRect` is the whole visible grid, not trimmed to the tile, for every tile, even one scrolled out of sight. So "is the pointer in this tile" checks `bounds` as well as `visibleRect`; `visibleRect` alone put the pointer in every tile, and every × showed when a scroll stopped. AppKit's own `.inVisibleRect` tracking areas aren't affected. Found by logging each tile's frame, visible rect and the pointer on every hover event.
- A tile's × button is always built and only faded in (opacity, hit testing), never added with `if isHovering`. Adding a SwiftUI `Button` makes SwiftUI rebuild the panel's focus order, and that took 8 to 13 ms per hover change, enough to drop frames while tiles scrolled past the pointer (measured with Instruments' SwiftUI template).
- A removed tile's picture comes from SwiftUI's `ImageRenderer`, which draws AppKit views as a placeholder, so it renders the tile's `face` alone, without the drag layer, and without the face's measuring, which would report frames from outside the tile. It doesn't inherit the panel's settings either (Reduce Transparency, which the glass reads), so `DissolveSnapshot` passes `@Environment(\.self)` on. That view only exists while the picture is taken: `@Environment(\.self)` in every tile would make each one depend on every setting.
- The dust's Metal view draws its first frame right away in `scatter`, with `presentsWithTransaction`, so the frame shows in the same Core Animation transaction as the rest of the window instead of at the view's next refresh. The tile hides only after handing over its picture, so for a moment both may show (they look the same), never neither.
- The dust's Metal layer gets its `contentsScale` from the window in `DustMetalView` itself (on joining the window, on a backing change, and before each scatter). The panel starts with a zero-size frame, and AppKit then leaves the layer at scale 0, which shows nothing at all, although every frame is drawn. Found with a small harness that captured its own window: the same panel set up at full size showed the dust.
- The dust draws the panel's outline into a mask texture and leaves out any grain outside it, rather than counting on SwiftUI's clipping, which may not apply to a Metal view. The view returns nil from `hitTest`, so clicks, hover and drops reach the tiles under it.
- Removed items leave the store only once their tiles say they've taken their pictures (`NotchViewModel.tileDissolved`, with a 250 ms fallback), not after a fixed hop to the main queue, which isn't sure to come after SwiftUI has drawn the change.
- SwiftUI's `clipShape` does clip a behind-window `NSVisualEffectView` to `NotchShape`, outward curves included, checked with a harness that captures its own windows over stripes. The curves lie outside the view's frame, so `PanelBackground` pads the material out by `NotchShape.openTopRadius` on each side before clipping; without that the ears would have no glass. The material view returns nil from `hitTest`, so clicks, hover and drops reach the shelf.
- Find the pointer's screen with `NSMouseInRect`, not `CGRect.contains`. The notch is at a screen's top edge, which `NSMouseInRect` counts as inside, so with a display placed above the MacBook the pointer resting on the notch still belongs to the built-in screen.
- After the panel collapses, it waits for the shrink animation to finish before it moves to another screen, so the animation doesn't end on the new one.
- Apple's docx reader (`NSAttributedString` with `.officeOpenXML`) drops pictures and headers, and keeps no links. Quick Look's converter keeps them: `qlmanage -p -o <folder> <file>` writes `<name>.qlpreview/Preview.html` with the pictures beside it. It writes nothing for a file it can't read, crashes on .odt, and only points back at .rtf and .txt files. It shows no window or Dock icon and doesn't change the front app. It leaves out the address of a picture a document links to on the web, and fetches nothing.
- `NSAttributedString.loadFromHTML` (WebKit) fetches remote pictures and stylesheets, and a Content-Security-Policy meta tag doesn't stop it (checked against a local server), so Quick Look's page has everything it would load from elsewhere taken out first (`withoutRemoteLoads`). It also draws every picture at its pixel size, ignoring a width and height given in CSS, attributes or a style, so `pictureSizes` reads Quick Look's sizes (in points, from its `.sN.sN.sN {width:…; height:…}` classes) and each picture goes back in as a fresh attachment holding just the image.
- Name the document type when reading with `NSAttributedString(url:options:)`. Left to guess, a file with HTML inside goes to the HTML reader, which only works on the main thread. The other readers, TextKit layout and drawing, attachment pictures included, all work off the main thread.
- Moving a file into Desktop, Documents or Downloads can wait on macOS asking for access, so `PDFConverter` does it off the main thread.
- PDFKit draws a page's thumbnail in the display's color profile once the page's text has been read, so a test that checks colors draws the page into an sRGB bitmap of its own.
