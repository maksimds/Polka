# Polka

Personal macOS menu bar app (Swift/SwiftUI + AppKit) that turns the MacBook notch into a hover-to-open shelf for files and links. Hover the notch to see stored items, drag files or links onto it to store them, drag items out into any app. Personal use, private repo.

`PolkaPRD.md` is the source of truth for goals, scope, requirements (FR-1 to FR-16), the data model, risks, and milestones. Read it before planning feature work.

## Settled decisions

- Dev machine: macOS 15.7.4, Xcode 16.3. Code is edited in VS Code; Xcode is for project settings and debugging.
- App deployment target: macOS 14.0. Swift 5 language mode. Bundle ID `com.maksimds.Polka`, personal team signing.
- Unsandboxed regular app with a Dock icon (it was a menu-bar-only agent, `LSUIElement = YES`, until 2026-11-12) and a `MenuBarExtra` Quit item. It's in the Dock and Cmd-Tab at all times. A click on the Dock icon only makes Polka the active app, which shows its menu bar (Polka, Edit, View, Window, Help); it opens no window. The Polka menu has Settings… (Cmd-comma), added with `CommandGroup(replacing: .appSettings)` since Settings isn't a SwiftUI `Settings` scene. Dropping files on the Dock icon isn't supported yet.
- Launch at login is an "Open at Login" checkmark in the menu bar menu and a switch in Settings, both backed by one `LoginItem` using `SMAppService.mainApp`. It's off until turned on. macOS keeps the setting (it's Polka's row in System Settings > Login Items), so Polka reads it again whenever a menu opens or Polka becomes the active app.
- Settings is an AppKit window (`SettingsWindowController`) holding a SwiftUI grouped form, opened from the menu bar menu ("Settings…", Cmd-comma) or the gear at the expanded panel's top right, which collapses the panel first. It is not a SwiftUI `Settings` scene, so Polka controls how it comes forward and goes away. It opens on the pointer's screen, and closing it hands focus back to the app you were in.
- The panel follows the pointer. There is one panel, and it moves to whatever screen the pointer is on while it's collapsed or open as a drop zone, but never while expanded or during a drag out. A screen without a notch gets the top-center pill (FR-14).
- The panel is drawn with `NotchShape`: rounded bottom corners, and top corners that curve outward into the screen edge (14 pt open). On a notch screen the closed shape has no outward curves and hides inside the notch. On a screen without one it's a 160 pt pill as tall as the menu bar, with a round bottom and 6 pt outward curves.
- Glass, after iOS 27's Liquid Glass (since 2026-11-12), drawn by hand in `UI/Glass.swift` because `glassEffect` needs macOS 26: a dark see-through body, a little lighter at the top, and a sharp 0.75 pt rim of light, brightest at the top, faint down the sides, with a reflection along the bottom. No blur, since the panel is a transparent window and a blur would show the desktop through it. No moving highlights. With Reduce Transparency on, glass turns solid grey and glass letters solid white. Glass is on the header's symbols and title, the tiles' remove badges, the message's ×, the link field's Add button, link icons and the drop zone's tray; file thumbnails, the eyes, the dashed box, the menu bar icon and the app icon are left as they are.
- The open panel's strip beside the notch shows "Polka" at the top left in glass letters (`GlassTitle`, 15 pt bold, half gloss: `GlassGlyph`'s `gloss` 0.5) and the Settings gear at the top right, both 2 pt below the middle of the notch's height (`NotchView.headerDrop`, shared with the link field's header). The strip is laid out on the tiles' grid (`ShelfGridView.columns`, the same 16 pt side inset, each end held to the 52 pt picture square `ShelfItemTile.imageSide`), so "Polka" starts where the first column's pictures start and the gear ends where the last column's pictures end. Left of the gear are the sort button (`arrow.up.arrow.down`) and then the + button (`plus`). Each of the three is a glass symbol itself, with no circle behind it (`GlassHeaderSymbol`, 14 pt semibold, gloss 0.85), 16 pt apart. The three end at that picture square's right edge and run a little into the fourth column, still clear of the notch. While any sort is on, the sort symbol is blue glass, #2881DB (`SortMenuButton.activeColor`, a fixed blue, not the system accent). Each frame is just the symbol, with an 8 pt larger click area, so the areas meet without overlapping.
- The shelf sorts like a Finder window's "Sort By". The sort button opens a menu headed "Sort By" with None, Name, Kind, File Type and Date Added, a checkmark on the current one, stored under `shelfSort` (None by default). Name is A to Z by the tile's title, Finder style (case doesn't count, "file2" before "file10"); Kind is folders, then files, then links, A to Z within each; File Type is folders, then one group per file extension from A to Z ("pdf" and "PDF" together, titled "PDF"), then files with no extension ("Other"), then links, A to Z within each; Date Added is newest first. Ties keep their order. Kind and File Type show their groups: each starts a new row under a title row (20 pt: the name in 11 pt semibold dim white, then a hairline to the end of the row), the title's ends lined up with the first and last columns' pictures like the header strip. 2 pt from a title to its tiles, 10 pt from a group's last row to the next title. Titles take no presses, so clicks and selection rectangles work there as on free space. Any choice but None keeps the shelf sorted through every change (new items land in their sorted spot, a renamed file or a link's arriving site name moves it), and tiles can't be moved by hand: the others don't make room, and a drop on the shelf glides the picture back to its own spot. Dragging out works as usual. Back on None, the tiles stay where the last sort put them, as in Finder, and new items arrive at the top left again. After a drop, the panel opens scrolled to show the new item if it sorted out of view.
- The + button opens a menu headed "Add New" with Link and File, placed like Sort By. Link turns the shelf into a link field (`NotchState.addingLink`): the shape shrinks with the opening spring to the shelf's width and just tall enough for the field (`linkEntrySize`), with "Add Link" where "Polka" was (glass letters too) and a glass × symbol where the gear was. The field (placeholder `https://example.com`) has its ends lined up with the first and last columns' pictures, and its Add button is clear glass with a dim label, turning #2881DB glass once the text is a web address. The menu items have no "…", at the user's request. `LinkInput` reads the text: no scheme means https (so "example.com" works, but then the host needs a dot or must be "localhost"), only http and https are accepted, and the scheme and host are lowercased and an empty path made "/", as browsers write them, so it matches the same page dragged in. Return adds it like a dropped link and grows the field back into the shelf, scrolled to the new tile. Text that isn't an address, or a link already on the shelf, gets a red line under the field, and the field stays. Esc, the ×, or a click in another app goes back without adding anything; moving the pointer away doesn't close it. File collapses the panel and opens the standard Open dialog (`NSOpenPanel`: files and folders, several at once, button "Add"). What's chosen is stored like dropped files, duplicates get the usual message, and the next hover opens scrolled to them.
- Motion: opening is one spring (0.42 s, bounce 0.12) shared by the shape and the shelf, which scales up from 0.92 at the top and fades in, so it grows out of the notch as one piece; closing is a quicker spring without bounce, with the shelf fading out first; the closed notch swells 14 × 4 pt in the last 100 ms of the hover delay with the same spring, so it flows into the opening (started earlier, it read as two separate steps); the open panel's shadow fades in once the opening settles; tiles grow in and shrink out as the grid reflows; a moved tile's neighbors slide aside once the pointer rests on a spot, and its dropped picture glides into its new spot; the delete button and selection fade; the drop zone has a glass tray icon (26 pt) that bounces once when a drag arrives and brightens while a drag is over the panel, and its eyes blink and grow 10% while a drag is over the panel. With Reduce Motion on, all of it becomes plain fades and the notch doesn't swell; tiles making room for a moved one jump to their places, a dropped tile fades in, and the eyes still follow the pointer but don't blink or grow.
- The app icon is the menu bar's `tray.full` symbol (semibold) as milky white glass on a dark graphite macOS tile (since 2026-11-12; it was near-black glass on a white tile before), at half gloss (`gloss` 0.5 in the script; full gloss looked like polished chrome): bright light reflections along the top of each stroke, grey edges where the glass reflects the dark room, a darker reflection of the tile along the bottom, and a soft shadow under it. `Design/make-app-icon.swift` draws it and writes every size into `AppIcon.appiconset`, so change the script and run it again rather than editing the PNGs.
- Storage is a plain JSON file for v1, not SwiftData.
- Hybrid storage: items reference the original by default, with an optional "keep a copy" for permanent items.
- Deleting an item never deletes the original file.
- New items are permanent by default. Temporary is opt-in per item, with the "Temporary" checkmark in the tile's right-click menu (the clock button in the tile's corner was removed), and the tile's detail line shows the time left. It lasts 24 hours unless changed in Settings (1, 6 or 12 hours, 1 or 3 days, 1 week), stored under `temporaryLifetimeSeconds`. A new lifetime only applies to items made temporary afterwards. A value set by hand with `defaults write` shows up as an extra choice.
- The drop zone has a pair of eyes just under the notch, above the dashed box (moved down to make room): 9.9 pt below the notch, then the eyes, then 11.88 pt down to the box. They're drawn after a face on a ball: two solid white capsules (12.72 × 43.2 pt, 28 pt apart) with no pupils, so looking means the whole face turning. Toward the pointer, the pair slides up to 6 × 3 pt, the eyes come closer together, the eye on that side shrinks a little, and a diagonal look tilts the head. A pointer 150 pt away turns them halfway. They blink every 2 s, the first a second after the drop zone opens, so a short drag still sees one. They read the pointer every frame (`TimelineView`), since no mouse-moved events arrive during a drag.
- An incoming drag from another app opens the drop zone after 0.5 s, or right away when the pointer reaches the notch's hover area (the Settings hover area). A drag that ends sooner never opens it. Opening instantly on every drag was distracting.
- Items stay on the shelf after being dragged out.
- Tiles can be moved around the shelf like apps on an iPhone home screen. Dragging a tile lifts it out, leaving a gap. Once the pointer rests on a spot for 150 ms, the other tiles slide aside to open the gap there, so sweeping across the grid doesn't shuffle every tile it passes. Every spot stays on the 5-column grid. A drop on the shelf puts the tile in the spot under the pointer, even if the tiles haven't made room yet, and saves the order (`sortOrder` in shelf.json). Dragging past the panel's edge makes it a normal drag out: the panel shrinks and the shelf goes back to its old order. Esc cancels. A selection moves as a group and lands side by side in its old order. New items still arrive at the top left. All of this is only while the shelf isn't sorted (Sort By None). Held within 16 pt of the grid's top or bottom edge (or past it, on the header), the grid waits 300 ms, then scrolls one row every 450 ms while there's more that way.
- The grid has 6 pt of room above its first row (`ShelfGridView.topInset`), outside the measured grid so the spot math is unchanged. A tall picture's remove badge rises above its tile, and on an unsorted shelf, with no group title above the first row, the scroll view's top edge cut it off. Two rows still fit without scrolling. Opening the panel and scrolling a row at a time go to the very top (`topID`) for the first row, rather than lining the first tile up with the edge, which would scroll the room away.
- File tiles use Quick Look's plain thumbnail. A dark drawing on a transparent background (at least 20% see-through, visible part at most 0.35 brightness, measured on a 32 × 32 copy) would vanish on the black shelf, so it's asked for again in Quick Look's icon mode, which draws it on a white page with a thin border like Finder. Icon mode isn't used for everything because it also frames photos.
- Hover is tuned in Settings: "Open after" (0 to 800 ms in 50 ms steps, 250 by default, stored as `hoverDelayMilliseconds`) and "Hover area" (Small is just the notch and is the default, Medium adds 12 pt at the sides and 4 below, Large adds 32 and 12; stored as `hoverArea`). Every area keeps 4 pt above the notch. Changes apply on the next hover.
- Multi-select works like Finder's icon view (FR-10): click selects one tile, Command-click adds or removes, Shift-click selects the range from the last plain click, a click on empty space clears, and a drag on empty space draws a selection rectangle. The rectangle selects every tile whose picture or name it touches, live; on its own it replaces the selection, with Shift it adds, with Command it flips what it covers. It stays inside the grid's visible area and doesn't scroll the grid. Dragging a selected tile carries the whole selection (missing files stay behind); dragging an unselected tile selects it alone. Selected tiles get a grey square behind the picture and an accent capsule around the name. Collapsing the panel clears the selection.
- A tile is pressed and hovered like a desktop icon: only what's drawn of it counts (`tileShape`), exactly, with no slack: the visible part of its picture, its name and its type line, each as wide as it's drawn, with the small gaps between them bridged so moving down the tile doesn't leave it. A link's icon or card counts whole; a file's thumbnail or icon counts without the see-through room a folder or document icon leaves around its drawing (`ShelfItemImages.visibleBounds`, measured once per image on a 64 × 64 copy). The rest of its column is free space, where a click clears the selection, a drag starts a selection rectangle, and the pointer doesn't hover the tile. Hover shows the remove badge, and while it shows, the badge's own area (26 pt with its margin) keeps the tile hovered, so the pointer can reach it where it hangs past the picture. The badge is the one iPhone puts on an app being deleted, with a × instead of a minus (`GlassRemoveBadge`: a 20 pt light grey glass circle with a dark ×, and a soft shadow), hanging over the top-right corner of the picture you can see, its middle 4 pt in from that corner; a tall page gets it on the page's corner, not the empty side of the picture square, and a folder gets it 15% of the picture's height lower (`folderBadgeDrop`), since the folder icon leaves the top of its square empty above the tab. During a trackpad scroll, tiles sliding under the resting pointer don't count as hovered, as in Finder; the tile under the pointer shows its × once the scroll comes to rest.
- Double-click opens an item (FR-9): a file in its default app, a folder in Finder, a link in the default browser. The panel collapses first. A file that can't be found does nothing.
- Opening, Quick Look and Get Info use a kept copy's original while it can still be found, since that's the real file, and Polka's copy otherwise.
- The right-click menu for files and folders is Quick Look, Get Info, a divider, Temporary and Keep a Copy (FR-16), then a divider and Remove. Links get Temporary and Remove. It's "Remove", not "Delete", because it only takes items off the shelf and never sends a file to the Trash, as with "Remove from Dock". Quick Look, Get Info and Remove act on the tile, or on the whole selection if the tile is in it; Remove then says how many ("Remove 3 Items"), since it can't be undone. Quick Look and Get Info are greyed out for an unavailable file. Temporary and Keep a Copy are checkmarks that act on the tile alone. The × button on a tile also removes just that tile. Quick Look and Get Info collapse the panel first. Quick Look is the system `QLPreviewPanel` (Space or Esc closes it, arrows move through several files). Get Info is Finder's own "Show Info" service, which needs no permission, unlike asking Finder through AppleScript.
- Drag out offers copy only, so a drop into Finder can never move the original. Over Polka's own panel the drag offers move (plus copy and generic, so holding Option or Command still moves the tile), so the pointer shows no green plus while tiles change places.
- Dropping something already on the shelf adds nothing and shows a message inside the panel. The message only closes with its × button. While it is up, the panel collapses after the pointer has been away for 3 seconds, and the message is still there on the next hover.
- Link tiles show the site's icon (from LinkPresentation, else the site's `/favicon.ico`) with glass built in like an iOS 27 app icon (`GlassPictureFinish`: a sheen across the top and a rim of light). How it sits in its 12 pt rounded square depends on the icon (`SiteIconStyle`, worked out once per icon file by `ShelfItemImages.siteIconStyle`). A large icon (96 pt wide or more) is a tile or a bare logo, told apart on a 32 × 32 copy: if more than half of its outer ring of pixels is see-through, it's a logo. A tile (GitHub's, Яндекс's: solid to the edges) fills the square. A bare logo (Shopify's bag) sits at 34 pt on dark glass (`GlassSurface` plus the finish); filling the square stretched it to the edges and cut it off. A favicon (under 96 pt, like LinkedIn's) is treated as a logo, at 28 pt, and never fills the square, even a solid one, since it would blur. A logo or favicon that's dark (`needsLightBackground`) goes on the light glass card (`LightGlassCard`) instead, since dark glass would hide it. Link tiles are titled "site/address path", like `GitHub/apple/swift`. The site name is the one the page declares (`og:site_name`), otherwise the capitalized domain (`Linkedin`). Requests are never disguised as a browser: a site that refuses Polka (LinkedIn) just gets the fallbacks.
- A file whose original can't be found stays on the shelf as "Unavailable" and can't be dragged out, but it can still be moved around the shelf. Its drag carries only a private pasteboard type (`ShelfDragSource.shelfOnlyType`) that no other app reads, and the panel doesn't shrink when it leaves, so it can come back. Anything Polka deletes from disk must be inside its own Application Support folder.
- "Keep a copy" lives in the tile's right-click menu, as a checkmark item for files and folders. Checking it copies the original in the background and points the item at the copy. Unchecking it goes back to the original and deletes the copy, but only if the original can still be found; otherwise the copy stays and a message says so.
- Copies live in `Copies/<UUID>/<file name>` inside Polka's folder, one folder per copy, so a copy keeps its real name when dragged out. A copy is deleted once no item uses it (removed, expired, or back on its original). Leftover folders are cleared at launch, except when shelf.json couldn't be read.
- Files that apps hand over as a promise (Photos, Mail, browser images) are written into a new copy folder and stored as copies with no original. They aren't checked for duplicates.
- A drop is read as real files first, then file promises, then links. A browser image offers both its address and a promise, and the image wins. A promise of a .webloc or .url file means the link.
- The Xcode project uses synchronized folders: new files under `Polka/Polka/` join the target automatically. Don't hand-edit `project.pbxproj` unless necessary.
- `buildServer.json` and `.vscode/settings.json` are local-only and git-ignored.
- The daily copy is `/Applications/Polka.app`, a Release build. The Desktop alias and Open at Login both launch it. Builds from Xcode or `xcodebuild` go to DerivedData and never update it, so `install.sh` at the repo root rebuilds it (into the git-ignored `Polka/build/`), replaces it, re-registers it with Launch Services (`lsregister -f`) so Finder and the Dock show its current icon instead of a saved one, and relaunches it. Open at Login points at whichever copy turned it on, so turn it on from the installed copy, not a Debug build.

## Layout

Layers follow the PRD. Keep them separated.

- `Polka/Polka.xcodeproj`
- `Polka/Polka/PolkaApp.swift`: app entry, `MenuBarExtra`, `@NSApplicationDelegateAdaptor`
- `Polka/Polka/App/`: `AppDelegate` (builds the store and helpers, owns the `LoginItem`, opens Settings, answers Quick Look's search for a controller), `Log` (os.Logger, one category per layer), `ExpirySweeper` (removes expired items), `LinkPreviewFetcher` (site names and icons for links), `CopyKeeper` (makes kept copies, receives promised files, deletes unused copies), `LoginItem` (Open at Login), `SettingsWindowController` (the Settings window), `FocusReturner` (hands focus back when Settings, Quick Look or the Open dialog closes), `QuickLookController` (Quick Look), `ItemOpener` (opens items, Finder's Get Info), `FileChooser` (the Open dialog for "Add New", "File")
- `Polka/Polka/Window/`: `NotchGeometry` (notch rect, top-center fallback, hover areas, which screen the pointer is on), `NotchPanel` (borderless non-activating `NSPanel`, all Spaces, never key except while the link field is up), `NotchPanelController` (state machine, hover, layout, following the pointer between screens, screen changes)
- `Polka/Polka/Input/`: `GlobalDragMonitor` (detects a drag starting anywhere via drag pasteboard `changeCount`, global mouse monitors, and a 0.05 s mouse-button poll for drag end that also reports the pointer while a drag goes on)
- `Polka/Polka/DragDrop/`: `DropTargetView` (AppKit `NSDraggingDestination` wrapping the SwiftUI content), `DropReceiver` (reads file URLs, links and file promises from the pasteboard), `ShelfDragSource` (`NSDraggingSource` owned by the panel controller, for drags out and tile moves), `TileDragView` (AppKit layer on each tile that starts drags, reports clicks and hover, and shows the right-click menu)
- `Polka/Polka/UI/`: `NotchView`, `NotchShape` (the panel's outline), `NotchViewModel` (`NotchState`, selection, the tile-move preview, adding from the + menu), `ShelfGridView`, `ShelfGridLayout` (the grid's fixed measurements, group titles included, the spot under the pointer, `ShelfGridArrangement`, the SwiftUI `Layout` that draws the grid from those numbers, and `ShelfGridGeometry`, where the grid is in the panel), `ShelfItemTile`, `ShelfItemImages` (icons, Quick Look thumbnails, the dark-on-transparent check, and site icons), `NoticeBanner`, `DropZoneEyes` (the drop zone's eyes, with `EyeGaze` and `EyeBlink`), `HeaderMenuButton` (a header symbol that opens an AppKit menu, shared by the sort and + buttons), `SortMenuButton` (the header's sort button and its "Sort By" menu), `AddMenuButton` (the + and its "Add New" menu), `LinkEntryView` (the link field the shelf turns into), `SettingsView` (the Settings form), `Glass` (the hand-drawn glass: `GlassSurface`, `GlassHeaderSymbol`, `GlassCloseMark`, `GlassGlyph`, `GlassTitle`, `GlassPictureFinish`, `LightGlassCard`)
- `Polka/Polka/Storage/`: `ShelfItem`, `ShelfStore`, `ShelfPersistence`, `JSONFileShelfPersistence` (shelf.json), `ShelfLocations` (Polka's Application Support folder), `ShelfCopies` (the Copies folder, and the only code that deletes copies), `ShelfSettings` (UserDefaults: temporary lifetime, hover delay, `HoverArea`, the sort), `FileReference` (bookmarks, moved and missing files), `LinkNaming` (link titles), `LinkInput` (typed text to a web address), `ShelfOrdering` (moving items to a new place in the list), `ShelfSort` (the "Sort By" orders; `ShelfStore` applies the current one in `commit`, which every change goes through). No AppKit or window dependencies, so it stays testable on its own.
- `Design/make-app-icon.swift`: draws the app icon. A standalone script outside the app target; how to run it is at the top of the file.
- `Polka/PolkaTests/`: unit tests. They run inside the app, and `AppDelegate` skips the panel while they do. A test that opens a real `NotchPanel` makes `AppLaunchTests` fail, because tests run in parallel.

## Build, run, test, logs

From `Polka/`:

- Build: `xcodebuild -project Polka.xcodeproj -scheme Polka -configuration Debug build -quiet`
- Run: `pkill Polka; open ~/Library/Developer/Xcode/DerivedData/Polka-*/Build/Products/Debug/Polka.app`
- Install the daily copy: `../install.sh`. Run it after each commit the user asks for (see Working style), or when they ask. It quits the copy they're using and replaces it.
- Unit tests: `xcodebuild -project Polka.xcodeproj -scheme Polka -destination 'platform=macOS,arch=arm64' test -only-testing:PolkaTests -quiet`
- Logs: `log stream --level debug --predicate 'subsystem == "com.maksimds.Polka"'`

Always build after changes to confirm it compiles. Hands-on testing (hover, drags, full-screen apps) is done by the user, who reports back.

## AppKit lessons (don't undo these)

- The panel can't become key (`canBecomeKey` returns `NotchPanel.acceptsKeyboard`, normally false). That is how it never steals focus. The one exception is the link field: in `.addingLink` the controller turns it on and makes the panel key. A `.nonactivatingPanel` can be the key window without activating Polka, so the other app keeps its menu bar. While it's key, the panel handles Cmd-X, C, V, A, Z and Shift-Z itself and swallows every other Command shortcut, since Polka's menus aren't on screen (Cmd-Q would quit a Polka you can't see). Leaving the field hands the keys back by ordering the panel out and straight back in (`giveKeyboardBack`); losing them to a click elsewhere collapses the panel. Don't make the panel key for anything else.
- SwiftUI `.onDrop` does not work here because it tries to make the panel key. Drops go through the AppKit `DropTargetView`.
- Never show an `NSAlert` or other system dialog from the panel, because it would activate Polka and take focus. Messages go inside the panel (`NoticeBanner`).
- Settings, Quick Look and the Open dialog (`FileChooser`) are the only times Polka activates itself (`NSApp.activate()`), because a window from an inactive app opens behind the app you're in and can't take keystrokes. All are windows you asked for, and the panel collapses before Quick Look or the Open dialog opens. Never activate Polka for the panel itself, the link field included. Opening an item or Get Info activates the other app (or Finder), never Polka.
- Quick Look finds the object that supplies its files by asking down the responder chain, starting at the key window. Polka never has a key window there, so the question reaches the app delegate. With SwiftUI that's SwiftUI's own delegate, which passes it on to `AppDelegate` (checked by `OpenAndInspectTests.theAppDelegateAnswersQuickLook`), which passes it to `QuickLookController`.
- The Settings window handles Cmd-W itself, since Polka's menu bar has no File menu and so no Close item.
- When Settings, Quick Look or the Open dialog closes, `FocusReturner` hands focus back with `NSRunningApplication.activate(from: .current)` to the last other app that was active. Otherwise Polka stays active with no window to type into. Quick Look and the Open dialog skip this while Settings is open.
- Any `NSWindow` that Swift keeps a reference to needs `isReleasedWhenClosed = false`, or closing it frees it twice and crashes.
- SwiftUI `Button` and tap gestures do work in the never-key panel. Only dragging needs AppKit.
- The panel is a fixed-size transparent window, and SwiftUI animates the shape inside it. `ignoresMouseEvents` is true only while the shape is notch-sized (`.collapsed` or `.draggingOut`), so clicks pass through. It also removes the window as a drop target, which is what lets a drag reach whatever is behind the panel.
- Inside drag callbacks, read the pasteboard right away but defer state changes by one run-loop tick. Changing state during the callback produces the system log "Reentrant message: kDragIPCCompleted ...".
- A file promise is the one exception: `receivePromisedFiles` is called inside `performDragOperation`, while the drag is still going. It only starts receiving; items are added when the files arrive.
- The tile's right-click menu comes from `TileDragView.menu(for:)`, because that AppKit layer covers the tile's picture and text and is what receives the click there. A right-click on the blank rest of the tile shows no menu, as on the desktop.
- The "Sort By" and "Add New" menus are `NSMenu`s opened by an AppKit layer over the header symbol (`HeaderMenuView`, in `HeaderMenuButton`), on mouse-down, not a SwiftUI `Menu`, so Polka places them: right under the symbol with the menu's right edge lined up with the symbol's. Their items run closures through `NSMenuItem(title:action:)`, which keeps its target alive in `representedObject`, since an item holds its target weakly. Opened to the right, it would hang past the panel's edge, and the panel collapses once the pointer has been out there. Menus don't need a key window, so they work in the never-key panel.
- A tile click is a press that ends without moving 4 pt, handled in `TileDragView.mouseUp` with the modifier keys read at mouse-down. A double-click is the second one, by `event.clickCount`, so it uses the double-click speed from System Settings.
- The tile's AppKit layer only takes presses on the tile's shape: its `hitTest` returns nil elsewhere, and AppKit then passes the press to whatever is behind, the grid's SwiftUI tap and drag gestures. Hover doesn't go through `hitTest`, and a tracking area is a rectangle, so it covers the whole column with `.mouseMoved` as well, and each move is checked against the shape; SwiftUI is told only when the answer changes. The panel's `acceptsMouseMovedEvents` is what lets those moves arrive while Polka isn't active. The tile measures its picture, name and type line with `onGeometryChange`, and passes its hover state back down (`isHoverShown`), since SwiftUI clears it on its own when the panel closes.
- Tiles report where their picture and text are into a plain class (`PressAreas`), not SwiftUI state. Those frames change on every scroll and nothing is drawn from them, so state would redraw the grid for nothing.
- Drags out start in the AppKit `TileDragView`. `ShelfDragSource` belongs to the controller rather than a tile, so AppKit always has a live source to report to.
- Polka must not treat its own drags as incoming: `GlobalDragMonitor` ignores them (`beginOwnDrag`, `endOwnDrag`). `DropTargetView` never stores a drag with a `draggingSource`, which AppKit only sets for drags from the same app; a drag from `ShelfDragSource` is a tile being moved instead.
- Moving tiles is one AppKit drag that does both jobs, not a custom drag inside the panel handed off to AppKit at its edge. Drag out starts exactly as before, and a drop back on the panel means "move here", as in Finder.
- The spot under the pointer comes from the grid's fixed measurements (`ShelfGridLayout`), not from where tiles are drawn. Tiles slide during the preview, and measuring targets that move makes them jump back and forth.
- The grid is drawn by a custom SwiftUI `Layout` (`ShelfGridArrangement`) from those same numbers, not by `LazyVGrid`, so what's drawn always matches the math, with or without group titles. It's one layout for every order (titles and tiles are siblings, told apart by `ShelfGridEntryKey`), so a tile stays the same view when the sort changes and slides to its new spot; `LazyVGrid` sections would rebuild every tile that changes group. Its cache remembers each view's last spot, and `updateCache` is deliberately empty, so a removed tile or title fades out where it was. Unlike `LazyVGrid`, it makes every tile (and starts its thumbnail) up front; fine for a personal shelf, which is built once at launch. The grid reports where it is (`ShelfGridGeometry`, a plain class like `PressAreas`) in SwiftUI's `.global` space, which in the panel is the hosting view, measured from the top left; `DropTargetView` converts AppKit's bottom-left points to it and back.
- `draggingUpdated` keeps arriving while the pointer holds still, which is what lets the preview settle and edge scrolling carry on without mouse movement.
- A dropped picture glides into place with `animatesToDestination`, set in `prepareForDragOperation`, then each dragging item's `draggingFrame` set in `performDragOperation` (enumerated as `NSPasteboardItem`, which visits every item, in the order the drag started with). Those frames are worked out during the drop without changing any state; the tiles move a tick later and stay hidden for 220 ms while the pictures land.
- The shelf's content is built once and stays mounted at full size in every state: hidden (opacity 0, no hit testing) and clipped to the notch unless `.expanded`. Opening only reveals it, so the first frames never wait for views, icons or thumbnails to be made, and the tile a drag out started from still exists when the drag ends. Because tiles outlive a closed panel, each tile clears its hover state when the panel closes, and the grid scrolls back to its first items on opening.
- Do no disk work as the panel opens. `prepareToOpen()` (remove expired items, refresh file references) runs when the hover delay starts and when an incoming drag starts, not when the drop zone finally opens.
- No mouse-moved events arrive during a drag, so check where the pointer is when one ends.
- Tracking areas need `.activeAlways`, since Polka is rarely the active app.
- Tracking areas report enter and exit when content scrolls under a pointer that isn't moving. `TileDragView` ignores entering between `NSScrollView`'s `willStartLiveScroll` and `didEndLiveScroll` notifications (a trackpad scroll, momentum included), then checks the pointer once. A mouse wheel posts neither, so hover still changes under one.
- Inside SwiftUI's scroll view, a tile's AppKit `visibleRect` is the whole visible grid, not trimmed to the tile, for every tile, even one scrolled out of sight. So "is the pointer in this tile" checks `bounds` as well as `visibleRect`; `visibleRect` alone put the pointer in every tile, and every × showed when a scroll stopped. AppKit's own `.inVisibleRect` tracking areas aren't affected. Found by logging each tile's frame, visible rect and the pointer on every hover event.
- A tile's × button is always built and only faded in (opacity, hit testing), never added with `if isHovering`. Adding a SwiftUI `Button` makes SwiftUI rebuild the panel's focus order, and that took 8 to 13 ms per hover change, enough to drop frames while tiles scrolled past the pointer (measured with Instruments' SwiftUI template).
- Find the pointer's screen with `NSMouseInRect`, not `CGRect.contains`. The notch is at a screen's top edge, which `NSMouseInRect` counts as inside, so with a display placed above the MacBook the pointer resting on the notch still belongs to the built-in screen.
- After the panel collapses, it waits for the shrink animation to finish before it moves to another screen, so the animation doesn't end on the new one.
